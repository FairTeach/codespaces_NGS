# Challenge 2 — Omics Project Proposal (Short Format)
## Master in Research and Innovation in Biotechnology
### Module: “Challenges in Biotechnology”

### Objective

Design a focused and feasible research proposal that applies omics technologies to a specific biological, biomedical, or biotechnological question. Use **two or three review articles** from the [article selection list](https://docs.google.com/spreadsheets/d/1mZdAAMBhLZ1a-GPtAdsS6eO52a1VSLMeDBJUzhkNeM4/edit?usp=sharing) as the scientific starting point, and integrate their ideas into a coherent proposal.

The assignment develops scientific reasoning and project-design skills. It is not a full grant application, and students are not required to perform the proposed experiments or generate new data.

### Assignment

- Work in your assigned team.
- Select **two or three review articles** from the provided list. You may also cite relevant primary research papers to support your rationale, study design, and methods.
- Formulate a specific research question, a testable hypothesis, and a small set of objectives.
- Propose a feasible study design. **Materials and Methods is the central section** and should explain how the objectives will be addressed.
- Submit a **12–15 page report**, excluding references, and give a **12–15 minute presentation**, followed by questions.

### Required report structure

| Section | What to include | Suggested length |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Title and Summary** | A clear title and a 200–250 word summary stating the problem, rationale, hypothesis/objectives, main approach, and expected contribution. | ½ page |
| **1. Introduction and Background** | Explain the biological or clinical problem; synthesise the selected reviews and relevant literature; identify the knowledge gap and justify why it matters. | 2–3 pages |
| **2. Research Question, Hypothesis, and Objectives** | State one focused research question, a testable hypothesis, and 2–3 specific objectives that follow logically from the gap. | 1 page |
| **3. Materials and Methods** | Describe the study design, samples/model or public dataset, omics technologies and rationale, main experimental or computational workflow, controls, analysis plan, and validation strategy. Explain feasibility and address relevant ethics, privacy, and reproducibility considerations. | 5–6 pages |
| **4. Expected Results and Interpretation** | Describe the results the study could produce, how they would address the objectives, how they would be analysed, and plausible alternative outcomes or interpretations. Do not present invented data as real. | 2 pages |
| **5. Significance, Innovation, and Limitations** | Explain what is novel or useful about the approach, its potential scientific or translational contribution, and its main limitations. | 1 page |
| **6. Conclusions** | Briefly restate the proposal’s contribution and the most important next step. | ½ page |
| **References** | List the selected reviews and all other cited sources in one consistent citation style. | Not included in page count |

Use the suggested lengths as guidance and keep the report within **12–15 pages**, excluding references. Include **4–5 relevant figures or tables**, with at least one original, clearly labelled workflow diagram illustrating the proposed methods. All figures and tables must be referred to in the text and include captions and source credits where applicable.

### Scope and feasibility

Keep the project focused enough to explain and justify within the page limit. A proposal may use one or more complementary omics approaches, but each must serve a clear purpose in answering the research question. A defined sample group, model system, or existing public dataset is preferable to an overambitious study.

Explain the main study-design choices, key analysis steps, controls, and how findings would be validated. Briefly address feasibility, limitations, and relevant ethical or data-protection issues. Do not claim access to samples, facilities, or datasets without identifying a plausible source.

The report does **not** require a funding strategy, budget, detailed work packages or Gantt chart, team biographies, or an extensive risk-management plan.

### Presentation

Present the problem and knowledge gap, research question and objectives, proposed methods and workflow, expected results, and significance and limitations. The presentation should last **12–15 minutes**, followed by questions. Each team member should contribute. Include at least one original slide showing the proposed workflow.

### Evaluation

| Criterion | Weight |
| --- | ---: |
| Research question, rationale, and synthesis of selected articles | 20% |
| Clarity, coherence, and feasibility of Materials and Methods | 40% |
| Expected results, interpretation, significance, and conclusions | 20% |
| Scientific writing and communication, figures, and references | 20% |

The criteria above apply to the written report; the oral presentation is assessed separately as part of the module grade. Teams should state each member’s contribution in a brief note at the end of the report.

### Submission

Submit the report as Word or PDF and the presentation as PowerPoint, following the deadline and platform announced in class. Use a consistent citation style and cite all external sources.

**End of Assignment**
